# Competitor Lenses

Use this reference to avoid shallow "brand ranking" analysis.

## 1. Pick buckets first

Choose 2 to 4 buckets before choosing product names:

- direct competitor
- adjacent workflow product
- substitute platform
- governance, tracing, or compliance layer

This prevents the analysis from missing products that shape user expectations even if they are not identical.

## 2. Suggested comparison dimensions

Use only the dimensions that matter for the product, but prefer this order:

1. target user and core job
2. execution model
3. state, memory, and resumability
4. coordination, handoff, or approval model
5. governance, permissions, and auditability
6. observability, replay, and debugging
7. portability and lock-in
8. onboarding and rollout friction
9. enterprise readiness
10. pricing or deployment constraints when visible

## 3. Questions that expose real differences

- Does this product optimize execution speed or coordination quality
- Does it depend on a hosted platform, or can it live inside the repo and workflow
- Can the team explain who changed what, when, and why
- Does it help only one agent, or a mixed human plus agent team
- Does it reduce review work, or create more reconciliation work
- What does it assume about trust, permissions, and data access
- What does it make easier that your product still makes manual

## 4. Converting findings into product decisions

After each competitor, write three short judgments:

- `validated strength`: what they prove users want
- `tradeoff`: what they likely make heavier, more rigid, or more locked-in
- `borrow`: what your product should adapt

## 5. Selection rule

Pick the smallest competitor set that can reveal:

- where the market has already converged
- what is still open territory
- what would make your product look clearly better
- what must change before your differentiation is credible
